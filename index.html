Laifen AutoCurl

Automatic curling iron · made for easier mornings

There are mornings when spending half an hour carefully curling your hair sounds reasonable. Then there are mornings when you're getting dressed, checking the time, looking for your keys, and trying to make both sides of your hair look vaguely related.

That's the situation where an automatic curling iron starts to make sense.

The Laifen AutoCurl is a newly launched 32 mm automatic curler built to take over one of the most repetitive parts of styling: wrapping hair around the barrel. Place a section into the chamber and the tool detects it, draws it around the barrel, and creates the curl without requiring you to twist your wrist through every section.

For someone searching for an easy automatic curling iron for everyday use, that simplicity is the main attraction. The more interesting question is whether the rest of the AutoCurl is equally practical once it becomes part of your normal morning.

Getting More Consistent Curls

Using a regular curling iron isn't particularly complicated once you've learned how. Getting the same result on every section is harder.

One curl gets wrapped tightly. The next section is larger, so it comes out looser. Your right hand naturally holds the iron differently when you move to the opposite side of your head. By the time you reach the back, you're working partly by feel.

AutoCurl reduces some of those variables.

Its 32 mm barrel stays the same from section to section, while the automatic mechanism handles the wrapping action. The tool can detect hair placement in around 0.5 seconds and begin drawing the strand into position.

That doesn't mean every curl will magically look identical. Section size, temperature, hair texture, and how long the curl is allowed to set still matter.

What changes is the physical movement.

Instead of manually deciding how tightly to wrap every strand, you're giving the same job to the same mechanism each time. For someone who struggles to make the left and right sides of their hair look balanced, that's useful.

The directional sensing system helps here too. AutoCurl can recognize how hair is placed into its dual chambers and manage curling direction automatically.

There's also Manual Mode when you want more control.

So you aren't giving up the ability to make styling decisions. You're simply removing some of the repetitive hand movements that can make the finished result inconsistent.

Styling the Back of Your Head Gets a Lot Less Awkward

The front pieces are rarely where a curling iron earns its keep. You can see what you're doing, your hands are in a comfortable position, and it's relatively easy to keep the hot barrel away from your face. Move behind your ears and toward the back of your head, and the whole process becomes less graceful. That's where AutoCurl's enclosed design starts to feel more useful than it looks in product photos.

With a conventional wand, you're holding an exposed heated barrel while manually wrapping hair around it.

That requires some spatial awareness when you can't clearly see your hands. It's easy to touch the barrel with a finger or bring it closer to your neck than intended.

AutoCurl places the 32 mm heated barrel inside a protective curling chamber.

You still need to position the tool correctly, but you aren't wrapping the strand around an exposed hot surface with your fingers. Place the section into the opening and let the mechanism do the rotation.

The tool weighs 388 grams without the cord, which also matters when you're holding it behind your head for several minutes.

Then there's tangle protection.

Automatic wrapping sounds convenient until you imagine your hair getting caught halfway through. Laifen designed the AutoCurl to monitor resistance while the strand is being drawn around the barrel. If it detects abnormal pulling, the system can stop and reverse instead of continuing to tighten.

You should still brush out tangles beforehand and avoid feeding oversized sections into the chamber. Automation works better when you give it clean sections to work with.

But for the sections you can't see particularly well, having the barrel enclosed and the wrapping handled automatically removes some of the most awkward parts of the job.

3 Temperatures Keep Things Simple

Some styling tools come with so many settings that choosing one becomes part of the morning routine. AutoCurl goes in the opposite direction. There are three temperature levels: 160°C (320°F), 180°C (356°F), and 200°C (392°F). That's enough range to adjust how much heat you're using without turning the handle into a control panel.

The AutoCurl uses dual ceramic PTC heating elements and takes around 50 seconds to heat up.

Once it's hot, Laifen's PID temperature-control system monitors the temperature up to 1,000 times per second. The purpose is to keep heat relatively stable while you're moving from one section to another rather than letting the barrel repeatedly overshoot and cool.

That matters because consistency isn't only about how the hair gets wrapped.

If one section is curled at a noticeably different barrel temperature from the next, the result can change even when everything else stays the same.

The tool also produces up to 200 million negative ions during styling. Laifen uses the ionic system to address static and help create a smoother-looking finish.

Of course, this is still heat styling. The temperature-control system doesn't make sensible heat choice unnecessary.

The better approach is to use the temperature that works for your hair rather than automatically reaching for 200°C because it's the highest option.

Once you've found a temperature and section size that produce the result you like, the AutoCurl becomes fairly repetitive in a good way: section, place, curl, release, repeat.

Hair length, thickness, section size, experience, and the type of curl you want all matter. Someone who's been using a traditional wand for ten years may move through their hair incredibly quickly. A beginner might spend much longer fixing uneven sections. The more useful comparison is how much work each tool asks you to do for every curl.

32 mm Barrel Tells You Who This Is Really For

Before buying AutoCurl, there's one specification worth considering more carefully than the motor or sensor speed: 32 mm.

That's the barrel size, and it has a direct effect on the type of style the tool is built to produce.

A 32 mm—or approximately 1.25-inch—barrel naturally lends itself to larger curls, loose waves, and softer shapes. After cooling, the curls can be left more defined or separated for a relaxed finish.

What it isn't designed for is tiny, tightly wound ringlets.

That's an important distinction because the AutoCurl doesn't use interchangeable barrels. You're buying into a particular style of curl.

For many people, that's exactly the point.

If loose curls are what you create most often anyway, there's little benefit in storing five different barrels you rarely touch. A fixed design also allows the automatic chamber to be built around one barrel size rather than accommodating a collection of attachments.

Hair length is another consideration.

Laifen recommends the AutoCurl for hair between approximately 25 and 60 cm, or 9.8 to 23.6 inches. Very short hair may not provide enough length for the chamber to wrap effectively, while extremely long hair falls outside the recommended range.

For medium and longer hair, however, the combination makes sense: a larger barrel, automatic wrapping, and a curl shape intended to look soft rather than overly tight.

It's worth knowing that before buying because AutoCurl isn't trying to be every curling iron for every hairstyle.

It's much more specific than that.

An Everyday Tool Than a Special-Occasion Gadget

A styling tool can have impressive technology and still end up forgotten in a drawer if it's annoying to use. AutoCurl's better features are arguably the ones that make it easier to pick up regularly: relatively low weight, a quick heat-up, simple temperature choices, an enclosed barrel, and automatic shut-off. None sounds revolutionary alone. Together, they determine whether using the tool on a Tuesday morning feels reasonable.

At 388 grams without the cord, AutoCurl isn't particularly cumbersome to hold.

It also supports 100–240V dual voltage. If you travel internationally, that means the electrical system itself can accommodate different supported voltages without a separate voltage converter. You'll still need the correct plug adapter where necessary.

The safety system includes a 60-second sleep function and 30-minute automatic shut-off.

That last one is easy to appreciate if you've ever left the house, made it halfway down the street, and suddenly wondered whether your curling iron was still switched on.

The newly launched AutoCurl is currently offered in Pink and White, keeping the product line fairly simple.

And unlike a large multi-styler system, there isn't a box full of attachments to organize. The focus stays on automatic curling.

That's consistent with the broader product approach from Laifen. The company tends to put quite a lot of engineering into personal-care devices while keeping the way you interact with them relatively straightforward.

AutoCurl follows the same idea. Underneath, there are sensors, servo controls, temperature monitoring, and an ionic system.

From the user's side, the routine is basically: put the hair in and make a curl.

So, Who Should Actually Buy It?

AutoCurl isn't automatically a better choice than every curling wand or air styler. It solves a particular set of problems, and whether those problems sound familiar will tell you more than a long specification comparison.

It makes the most sense if you regularly wear loose curls or waves and find manual wrapping tedious.

It's also worth considering if you're relatively new to curling your own hair. There's still a technique to sectioning and positioning the tool, but you don't have to master the wrist rotation required by a conventional wand.

People who struggle with the back sections of their hair are another obvious audience. The enclosed chamber and automatic wrapping can make those harder-to-see sections less awkward.

Frequent travelers may appreciate the 388-gram body and dual-voltage support as well.

On the other hand, experienced stylists who like changing barrel sizes and wrapping techniques may find a traditional system more flexible.

And if you want one expensive tool that dries, curls, smooths, and volumizes, a multi-styler is a different category and probably a better comparison.

AutoCurl works best when your requirement is much simpler:

You want curls. You usually want them loose rather than extremely tight. And you'd prefer the machine to handle the twisting.

Conclusion

The strongest argument for Laifen AutoCurl isn't that automatic curling is new. It's that the product takes several small annoyances associated with curling your own hair and tries to remove them at once.

The 0.5-second hair detection gets the wrapping started. Directional sensing reduces the need to manage every left and right curl yourself. The 32 mm barrel keeps the result focused on loose curls and waves, while three heat settings give you some control over temperature.

Then there are the features you hope you never need to think about: tangle detection that can stop and reverse the mechanism, an enclosed heated barrel, and automatic shut-off.

Put together, the AutoCurl feels less like a replacement for every styling tool you own and more like a shortcut for one specific job.

If you love manually shaping every curl, keep the wand.

If you'd rather spend less time twisting your wrist and more time getting on with your morning, AutoCurl's approach makes a lot more sense.
